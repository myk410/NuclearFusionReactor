# Nuclear Fusion Reactor — permanent research instructions

## Objective and boundaries
Investigate scientifically defensible pathways to sustained net-positive usable electrical energy across the entire fusion facility. Work independently across nuclear, quantum, particle, plasma and solid-state physics; chemistry and electrochemistry; thermodynamics and electromagnetism; materials, electrical and mechanical engineering; computational mathematics, statistics and energy conversion. Challenge conventional and unconventional proposals equally. Develop hypotheses, not hazardous reactor construction or operating instructions.

## Scientific integrity
Never fabricate evidence, citations, experiments or computations. Open original sources where possible; mark abstract-only, inaccessible, institutional announcement and unverified sources. Separate established principles, measured results, published projections, project calculations and hypotheses. Document contrary evidence, model limitations, uncertainty and errors. Mathematical consistency is not physical feasibility. Do not claim novelty without a prior-art search. Do not describe subjective scores or Monte Carlo scenario fractions as probabilities of technological success. Never claim a research stage complete without its acceptance evidence.

## Workflow and continuity
Read README.md, ProjectStatus.md, Progress/Continuation.md and ResearchIndex.md first. Inspect current git status and upstream before editing. Preserve existing work. Commit meaningful progress frequently, including unfinished research with explicit status. Update continuation and progress records at each checkpoint. Use small text files, standard-library code where practical, and no redundant backups, large dependencies or downloaded paper archives. This Git repository is the permanent workspace; do not duplicate it into other storage.

## Required sequence
1. Foundations: infrastructure, scientific standards, equations and baseline knowledge.
2. Comprehensive primary-source literature review, including magnetic/inertial/magnetized target fusion, tokamaks, stellarators, alternative confinement, Thunderbird/electrochemistry, muons, solid-state experiments, tunneling/screening, fuel cycles, stability, superconductors, conversion, heat management, irradiation and historical/abandoned approaches.
3. At least 30 scientifically distinct concepts, with mechanism and falsification distinctions. Label plant architectures separately from enabling subsystems and diagnostic research.
4. Preliminary evaluation, including counterarguments.
5. Reproducible non-operational mathematical/computational studies with units, assumptions, constants, sources, limiting cases and uncertainty.
6. Transparent comparative rankings and weight sensitivity.
7. Substantially deeper review of the leading ten, with new source evidence and revised calculations.
8. Independent skeptical scrutiny of leading concepts; do not call a single author's critique independent peer review.
9. Refine and retain rejected hypotheses with reasons; investigate new combinations when justified.
10. Comprehensive reports for the strongest five and an evidence-qualified recommendation. Never manufacture completion to fit one session.

## Concept dossier minimum
Each concept gets a unique C### identifier and a dedicated document with: name/ID; summary; principles; supporting literature; theoretical basis; mathematical justification; full system energy implications; assumptions; experimental evidence; contradictory evidence; scientific obstacles; engineering obstacles; open questions; comparative advantages; failure reasons; necessary breakthroughs; professional validation questions; scientific feasibility; engineering feasibility; evidence confidence. Add independent evidence quality, uncertainty, maturity, research potential, originality and dependence on undiscovered phenomena. Give score-specific reasoning and falsification tests. Identify shared dependencies and avoid counting minor variations toward the portfolio.

## Accounting and scoring
Define fuel-delivered, fusion, facility input, recoverable heat, gross electric, auxiliaries, losses, duty cycle and maintenance energy. Count startup, storage losses, downtime, fuel processing, cryogenics, pumping and replacement where material. Never equate target/plasma gain with net electric gain. Check energy conservation and avoid counting the same energy twice.
Score 0–10 with weights: physics 20%, net electricity potential 20%, evidence 15%, theory 10%, engineering 10%, unresolved barriers (higher means fewer/less severe) 10%, scalability 5%, materials/resources 5%, economics 5%. Overall = sum(weight percent × score / 10). Apply an explicit physics gate: a demonstrated violation is rejected regardless of arithmetic. Missing evidence is not itself a violation. Compare architectures separately from enablers. Scores are comparative judgments, not probabilities.
Maintain overall and independent rankings for plausibility, net-electric potential, evidence, unconventional promise, long-term research potential, barriers and validated potential impact. Record score changes and conduct weighting sensitivity. Do not penalize unknowns invisibly: document rationale and confidence.

## Identifiers and reproducibility
Use R### references, H### hypotheses, F### findings and C### concepts. Preserve source URLs/DOIs, access date, source type, actual access depth and supported claims. Every result links to code and inputs; deterministic seeds for stochastic studies. Preserve rejected hypotheses and corrections. Update master index, status, rankings and continuation with relevant files, blockers, completed work, next steps and a reusable continuation prompt.
