# Electrochemistry, screening and solid-state targets — CH001

R002 reports a 15(2)% relative D–D enhancement; R003 explicitly disclaims net energy. The useful cross-disciplinary question is whether electrochemistry changes the *spatial deuterium concentration sampled by the beam*, electronic screening, or both. A single reaction-rate ratio cannot identify the mechanism.

A beam-target yield per projectile has the reduced thick-target form Y=integral n_D(x) sigma(E(x)) dx, with dE/dx=-S_stop(E,x). Hence both density and stopping power are nuisance variables when inferring a screening potential. The integral is dimensionless: m^-3 × m² × m. Include incident-ion species fractions, evolving surface state and detection efficiency in inference. This project has not fitted Thunderbird raw data.

H001: perform a joint statistical inference using independent depth-profile constraints and stopping-power uncertainty. Reject a proposed screening explanation if it cannot predict held-out yield changes after density effects are accounted for. This is an analysis hypothesis, not a claim of discovering a new screening mechanism.

For beam recycling, let p be fusion probability per pass, E_b beam energy per projectile and rho the recoverable fraction of non-fusing projectile energy. Even optimistically omitting replacement of reacting particles, wall-power input per projectile is at least (1-rho)(1-p)E_b/eta_acc plus fuel loading and auxiliaries. A small relative increase in p cannot be equated to electrical breakeven. R008 similarly requires the entire photon-production chain to be counted.
