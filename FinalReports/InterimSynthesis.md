# Interim synthesis — no final discovery claimed

The current best-supported **research direction** is an integrated D–T magnetic-confinement comparison, retaining high-field tokamaks (C001) and optimized stellarators (C002) in parallel. Their 73.0 versus 72.5 scores do not resolve a winner. This is a preliminary prioritization based on reviewed evidence and accounting, not proof of net electricity or an assertion that the historically most-funded approach must prevail.

Indirect-drive ICF (C033) must remain a serious comparator because target gain is experimentally established. Spherical tokamaks and direct-drive ICF complete the current top-five screen, but fifth-place membership is sensitive to priorities. MagLIF and mirrors remain important alternatives; complete review may change the ordering.

Potentially high-value cross-disciplinary work includes:
- Simultaneous fuel-processing, exergy, shielding and maintenance accounting rather than isolated plasma-gain optimization.
- Density/screening identifiability for driven solids, with held-out nuclear-yield predictions.
- Charged-loss recovery coupled to plasma self-heating and actual converter spectra.
- Materials and exhaust choices evaluated through their effects on cycle-average electricity and fuel self-sufficiency.

The first calculations reveal accounting and inference constraints. They do not discover a new reaction, validate a proposed reactor or establish an absolute likelihood of success. A clear finding can be that a proposed inference fails, even when the experiment remains scientifically valuable.

Stages 2–10 are not complete. The five interim reports are preliminary decision records, not the requested eventual comprehensive final feasibility reports. Finish source retrieval, contradictory-evidence coverage, architecture-specific coupled models, material/fuel/cost inventories and independent expert scrutiny before issuing final conclusions.
