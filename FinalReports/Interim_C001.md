# Interim feasibility assessment — C001: High-field D–T tokamak

**Status: preliminary, not the project's final comprehensive report.** Date: 2026-11-24. Current plant rank: 1; comparative score: 73.0/100. Evidence confidence: moderate. Scores are judgments, not success probabilities. Full dossier: [C document](../TheoreticalConcepts/C001.md); focused audit: [leading-ten review](../Research/LeadingTen/C001.md).

## Scientific case
Confinement-integrated high-field D–T remains the first comparison baseline because its fusion mechanism has substantial experimental ancestry and the design path can be expressed without new fundamental phenomena. This does not resolve engineering integration.

The 2026 ARC V3A study states a new plant design and explicitly identifies unresolved physics risks. Its proposed electric output belongs in the projection column, not experimental evidence. Older ARC/SPARC numbers describe different devices and dates.

## Quantitative basis available now
The executed E001 balance is x=eta_t(c M+r/Q)-1/(eta_d Q)-a. Under the explicitly illustrative eta_t=.4, c=.95, M=1, r=0 and a=.08, Q_min=6.6667 at eta_d=.5, 22.2222 at .15 and 333.3333 at .01. These **are not parameters assigned to this concept**. They show why its actual driver, capture and auxiliary data must be obtained before calculating a plant-specific margin.

The statistical parameter boxes and ranking perturbations in [computational findings](../Simulations/Findings.md) are executed, reproducible scenario exercises. They cannot supply attainable Q, actual conversion efficiency, maintenance life or economic viability. No reactor-specific plasma simulation or neutron-transport calculation was run here.

## Strongest counterargument
The B^4 power-density argument hides fixed-beta and fixed-temperature assumptions. Increased field can change coil stress, shield geometry and achievable operating space; these must be constrained jointly.

Cryogenic load, centre/inboard shielding, blanket coverage, plasma radiation and replacement outages can overwhelm the apparent compactness benefit.

## What would change the decision
Reclassify below the stellarator or inertial routes if adequate confinement and exhaust require current-drive, radiation or outage burdens that prevent a robust net-electric region.

## Highest-value next investigation
Prioritize a single residual-tested coupled model linking transport, exhaust, recirculating drive, shield/magnet lifetime and breeding. Compare a larger, less stressed configuration against the compact optimum instead of presuming smaller is cheaper.

Write a residual-tested balance that links confinement uncertainty, current drive, exhaust and actual heat grades to the grid meter. Require empirical bounds rather than simultaneously selecting optimistic values.

## Integration and qualification gates
Before advancing to an experimentally defensible plant claim, establish a measured or independently validated confinement/loss basis, compatible exhaust and materials limits, a full electricity/fuel-processing ledger, and positive energy over maintenance cycles. For a D–T host, fuel self-sufficiency must include reserve inventory, decay, processing losses and deployment growth rather than a nominal TBR target alone. Report the physical uncertainty and the decision sensitivity separately.

Cost and materials-resource models have not been produced for this concept. No LCOE, deployment date, experimental endorsement or probability is provided. Shared sources and shared engineering dependencies mean alternatives are not independent draws from a success distribution.
