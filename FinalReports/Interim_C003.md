# Interim feasibility assessment — C003: Spherical tokamak with protected central column

**Status: preliminary, not the project's final comprehensive report.** Date: 2026-11-24. Current plant rank: 4; comparative score: 65.0/100. Evidence confidence: low. Scores are judgments, not success probabilities. Full dossier: [C document](../TheoreticalConcepts/C003.md); focused audit: [leading-ten review](../Research/LeadingTen/C003.md).

## Scientific case
The spherical tokamak uses a distinct geometric tradeoff: high beta and potential compactness against severe centre-column and shielding constraints. Its ranking above some alternative routes is provisional and especially sensitive to engineering assumptions.

The systems study discusses uncertainty when extrapolating confinement to highly radiative power-plant plasmas. Super-X tests are relevant subsystem evidence, but do not validate a protected central column or full breeding configuration.

## Quantitative basis available now
The executed E001 balance is x=eta_t(c M+r/Q)-1/(eta_d Q)-a. Under the explicitly illustrative eta_t=.4, c=.95, M=1, r=0 and a=.08, Q_min=6.6667 at eta_d=.5, 22.2222 at .15 and 333.3333 at .01. These **are not parameters assigned to this concept**. They show why its actual driver, capture and auxiliary data must be obtained before calculating a plant-specific margin.

The statistical parameter boxes and ranking perturbations in [computational findings](../Simulations/Findings.md) are executed, reproducible scenario exercises. They cannot supply attainable Q, actual conversion efficiency, maintenance life or economic viability. No reactor-specific plasma simulation or neutron-transport calculation was run here.

## Strongest counterargument
High beta and reduced size cannot be optimized separately from structural space, current drive and radiation-compatible confinement. A free optimizer can choose a favorable design by moving other uncertain variables.

Column replacement could dominate availability; extra shield or exhaust volume may remove the compactness advantage.

## What would change the decision
Demote if centre-column life, breeding and steady-state support cannot be simultaneously met. A future explicit robust-feasibility region could justify promotion; a free-parameter optimum cannot.

## Highest-value next investigation
Construct a radial-build and availability-constrained systems study and audit the confinement-scaling dependence in R029. Ask whether the viable envelope survives a conservative centre-column replacement schedule.

Sweep a constrained radial-build budget and current-drive burden, then propagate lifetime-driven outages. Evaluate whether a broad feasible region survives rather than reporting a single optimum.

## Integration and qualification gates
Before advancing to an experimentally defensible plant claim, establish a measured or independently validated confinement/loss basis, compatible exhaust and materials limits, a full electricity/fuel-processing ledger, and positive energy over maintenance cycles. For a D–T host, fuel self-sufficiency must include reserve inventory, decay, processing losses and deployment growth rather than a nominal TBR target alone. Report the physical uncertainty and the decision sensitivity separately.

Cost and materials-resource models have not been produced for this concept. No LCOE, deployment date, experimental endorsement or probability is provided. Shared sources and shared engineering dependencies mean alternatives are not independent draws from a success distribution.
