# Alternative confinement and historical lessons

**Principles.** Open mirrors trade axial losses against simpler access; FRCs trade compact high-beta geometry against orbit and stability requirements; pinches trade external-coil complexity against current-driven instability. All obey collisional and energy limits.

**Accomplishments.** R013 reports coincident electron-temperature and neutron observations. R019 reports beam-wave interaction with fuel ions. R014 reports a small plasma-energy conversion demonstration and explicitly states that most recovered power came from supplied heating, with no net-electric demonstration.

**Limitations.** None of these observations substitutes for complete source-to-electric gain. Nonthermal distributions have a maintenance cost. A recovered end-loss particle is still energy lost from the confined plasma unless another channel sustains it.

**Open questions.** Is stabilization effective in the burning regime? Does conversion change the boundary conditions required for confinement? Are measured gains thermonuclear, beam–target, or mixtures?

**Competing explanations.** Fast-ion tails can enhance neutron rates without raising bulk ion temperature. A current collected in an output circuit can be recycled input energy rather than fusion energy. Independently calibrated distribution and energy diagnostics are essential.

**Historical evidence.** R030 explicitly separates compression-induced heating from confinement degradation. It does not prove every modern FRC proposal fails, but invalidates inference from heating alone. A systematic account of historical mirror, reversed-field-pinch, spheromak and electrostatic failures is outstanding.

**Current correction.** R028 corrects a root-finding method that admitted points without power balance; revised operating points still support the authors' theoretical Q>5 conclusion. This is a model-integrity lesson, not a measured gain result or wholesale refutation of mirrors.

**Connections.** Require solver residuals and independent algebraic balances in every future nonlinear model. Treat direct recovery and plasma self-heating as coupled. Do not automatically resurrect an abandoned geometry merely because magnets improved: identify the original limiting mechanism and show how new evidence changes it.
