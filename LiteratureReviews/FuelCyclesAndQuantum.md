# Fuel cycles, tunneling and selective energy transfer

**Principles.** A fusion reaction's released binding energy does not specify its useful rate or net plant output. Quantum tunneling enters cross sections; screening can alter effective collision probabilities but is not a source of nuclear energy. The rate distribution must be integrated for each fuel and actual particle population.

**Evidence.** D–T has extensive experimental evidence in the ledger (R018, R022). R016 supplies a theoretical mechanism for redistributing alpha energy. This is not evidence of proton–boron plant gain. Evaluated cross sections have not been ingested, so no quantitative comparison of fuel-cycle Lawson minima is asserted.

**Limitations.** D–T entails neutron/material and fuel-breeding burdens. D–D entails additional branches and secondary products. D–He3 entails demanding conditions and He3 supply; p–B11 entails demanding distribution/radiation balance. Calling an advanced primary branch aneutronic does not justify zero secondary neutrons or activation.

**Open questions.** Can ion-selective heating persist against collisions at an acceptable electrical cost? Does direct alpha extraction remove heating needed for burning? How do ash and impurities change radiative loss and usable particle energy?

**Competing theory.** R011 is a lead to non-equilibrium power bounds, while R016 explores selective redistribution. Reconcile their assumptions rather than quoting either as a universal verdict. A full kinetic treatment must include entropy production, wave growth/damping, source efficiency and all escaping particles.

**Historical failure mode.** Promoting a cross-section maximum, one tail enhancement or a large nuclear energy release as sufficient for breakeven omits stopping and sustainment costs. This is a logical failure, not a claim that every nonthermal experiment has failed in the same way.

**Recent work and connections.** Better superconductors, beams and wave diagnostics may change attainable regimes without changing nuclear cross sections. Electron screening studies in solids could improve low-energy nuclear inference; transferring that result to burning-plasma performance requires a separate calculation.

**Outstanding.** Add original muon cycle data, evaluated reaction branches, radiation functions, D–He3 resource constraints and impurity-sensitive bremsstrahlung before making fuel-by-fuel numeric claims.
