# Inertial and magnetized-target fusion — initial critical synthesis

**Principles.** Inertial confinement relies on significant burn before disassembly; magnetization can alter conductive losses. Compression work, ignition drive and chamber reset all consume energy.

**Accomplishments.** R022 reports target gain greater than unity in indirect-drive experiments. R001 gives a later 2025 laboratory yield. R026 concerns hydro-equivalent direct-drive scaling rather than ignition achieved at the experimental scale. R012 identifies thermonuclear MagLIF conditions.

**Limitations.** Single-shot yield does not establish driver wall efficiency, repetition, target supply, optics survival or average availability. Magnetized targets add flux retention, mix and kinetic-scale questions. Liquid-liner and heavy-ion plant projections need more architecture-specific primary evidence than obtained here.

**Unresolved questions.** Does realistic instability/mix spoil the energy advantage of a different driver? Can target quality be maintained at industrial production rate? Can recovery schemes retain compression energy without disturbing plasma formation?

**Competing interpretations.** Neutron spectra and yield may include nonthermal populations; apparent temperature and integrated energy are distinct. Simulations with clean symmetric targets are not interchangeable with measured mixed targets.

**Historical setbacks.** R025 makes mix a specific adverse effect rather than an abstract caveat. Early failed ignition predictions require a dedicated source review; that task remains open.

**Recent development.** New coupling and gain reports justify revisiting old driver alternatives, but transferring an indirect-drive success to direct drive or fast ignition requires a new energy/coupling chain.

**Connections.** C004–C009 and C033 share repetition and chamber/material dependencies. Power electronics, target-manufacturing energy, exergy and statistical target-quality control may matter as much as peak fusion yield. These routes remain correlated and should not be counted as independent chances.
