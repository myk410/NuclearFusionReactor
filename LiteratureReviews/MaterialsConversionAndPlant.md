# Materials, conversion and complete plant integration

**Principles.** Thermal efficiency is constrained by the temperature of each heat stream and sink. Pumping and refrigeration consume useful electricity; a superconducting coil is not a zero-consumption magnet system. Irradiation changes useful component life and hence cycle-average energy.

**Evidence.** R004 and R005 frame plant integration gaps; R017 supplies an exhaust-subsystem result. R015 identifies primary MHD flow work. R024 calculates blanket/fuel-cycle feasibility at limited fidelity. These justify detailed subsystem research, not an assumption that integration is solved.

**Limitations.** Higher coolant temperature may improve conversion while worsening corrosion or structural creep. A thicker shield may extend magnet life while enlarging the device or reducing breeding space. Electrically insulated liquid flow may reduce MHD pumping while impairing heat transfer or reliability. A larger radiating edge may spare walls while reducing fuel density or confinement.

**Open questions.** Which favorable subsystem values can coexist? Can irradiated joints, insulation and permeation barriers meet lifetime and maintenance constraints? What loads remain during forced outages? How is target or fuel processing energy allocated?

**Competing explanations.** Lower local wall heat can mean energy was radiated elsewhere, not reduced system loss. Improved availability may reflect a different operating duty rather than improved component reliability. High gross cycle efficiency may omit pump/compressor loads or assume all heat at the hottest available temperature.

**Historical limitation.** Sparse fusion-spectrum qualification makes extrapolation from fission or charged-particle irradiation uncertain. A quantitative historical material-failure review remains outstanding rather than being replaced by generic lifetime assertions.

**Recent modelling.** R024 compares breeder options; its TBR values are conditional model outputs with unresolved ports, integration and operating losses. Fuel self-sufficiency needs a time-dependent inventory balance, not TBR alone.

**Connections.** Prioritize C019–C029 and C032 alongside source concepts. These interventions may help multiple host architectures, but their scores are conditional and their savings cannot be added without checking shared constraints. A real plant assessment needs cost, neutron transport, actual heat grades and full-cycle availability data absent from this first pass.
