# Foundations — P001

## Fusion production versus losses
For distinguishable reacting species a and b in an uncorrelated homogeneous population, reaction-rate density R=n_a n_b <sigma v> [m^-3 s^-1]. Identical reactants require a factor 1/2. Fusion power density is R E_f [W m^-3] with E_f in joules; 1 eV=1.602176634e-19 J exactly. Reactivity must use the actual velocity distribution, not an assumed temperature for a beam.

For a quasineutral, fully ionized, equimolar D–T plasma with equal ion/electron temperature T and total ion density n, W/V=3 n k_B T, R=n²<sigma v>/4. A simple energy condition is f_alpha R E_f >= 3 n k_B T/tau_E + P_rad/V. Ignoring radiation gives n tau_E >=12 k_B T/(f_alpha <sigma v> E_f). This is a deliberately idealized alpha-heating condition, not a universal plant criterion or a validated numerical Lawson curve. Electron/ion disequilibrium, impurities, transport and ash change the result.

For nonresonant low-energy charged reactions, sigma(E)≈S(E)/E exp[-2 pi eta_C(E)], where eta_C=Z_a Z_b alpha c_light/v. S(E) contains nuclear structure. Screening is often parameterized as an effective energy shift U_e; a relative rate change does not determine U_e without controlling target density and energy loss. Chemical energy scales can alter target state without supplying MeV collision energies. A lattice that remains cool can still be bombarded by energetic particles.

## Hard constraints and useful scalings
At fixed pressure profile, beta=2 mu_0 p/B², and fixed temperature/distribution, fusion power density scales approximately as beta² B^4. This heuristic omits confinement, blanket thickness, coil stress and shielding, and cannot be extrapolated without bounds. Magnetic energy density and mechanical stress scale with B², so field is not a free lever.

Thermal conversion obeys eta<=1-T_c/T_h for ideal reservoirs; temperature must be in kelvin. Real multi-stream heat capture lies below the reversible limit. Semiconductor band gaps, chemical catalysis and superconductivity do not abolish Coulomb, entropy or radiation losses.
