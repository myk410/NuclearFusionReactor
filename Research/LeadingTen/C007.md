# Focused leading-candidate audit — C007: Heavy-ion driven inertial fusion

This is a deeper **first audit**, not a completed extensive feasibility review. Current comparative score: 54.0/100. It is a single-analysis critical review, not independent peer review. See the full [concept dossier](../../TheoreticalConcepts/C007.md).

## Additional source question
References: R027. Exact URLs and access limits are in [Bibliography](../../References/Bibliography.md).

NDCX-II is relevant accelerator pulse-compression evidence. It is not an integrated reactor driver at full target requirements, and a heavy-ion fusion-reaction paper about heavy nuclei is not evidence for heavy-ion-driven light-fuel power.

## Challenge to the theoretical foundation
Good accelerator wall efficiency is insufficient if target delivery needs loss-inducing focusing or propagation, or if gain does not survive a real chamber.

## Required mathematical investigation
Construct a coupled driver-to-target efficiency budget with explicit transport and chamber penalties and a cost/throughput ledger. Only then compare eta_d Q to the electrical threshold.

The executed E001 model supplies only a necessary electrical threshold. It supplies no architecture-specific confinement result. A future coupled model must include input bounds, units, source provenance, numerical residuals, sensitivity and an independent algebraic balance. Matching one published optimum is inadequate calibration.

## Skeptical integration review
Large accelerator infrastructure and focus/transport tolerances may dominate capital and availability even with promising pulse physics.

Require a common comparison service: delivered electricity across at least a maintenance cycle. Report net on-state and mean net separately. State whether target supply, cryogenics and fuel processing are on-site or external; externalizing the load cannot improve the expanded system balance.

## Decision and outstanding evidence
Keep evidence score conservative until modern original driver-scale experiments and target-coupling studies are reviewed.

Outstanding: full relevant papers and supplements; a contradictory-results search; calibrated parameter distributions; cost/resource inventory; a robust region of coupled energy closure. Until these exist, no plant-specific success probability or comprehensive feasibility conclusion is justified.
